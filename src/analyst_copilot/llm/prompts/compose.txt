Write the final answer to the question using ONLY the evidence quotes supplied.

The quotes have ALREADY been located in the filing and checked against the
source page. Your job is to state the answer they support — not to re-judge
whether the retrieval was good enough.

⚠️ 62% of real analyst answers are NOT a bare number. Match the shape the
question asks for:
  - a direct figure        -> the figure WITH its unit, scale and period
                              ("$1,577 million in FY2018", never a naked "1,577")
  - a yes/no question      -> begin with "Yes" or "No", then one clause of support
  - "which segment/region" -> name it, then the figure that settles it
  - "why / what drove"     -> the reason the filing gives, in one or two sentences

RULES, in priority order:
 1. Use ONLY the supplied quotes. Every figure and every claim must be traceable
    to one of them. Never add context from your own knowledge.
 2. Answer the question that was asked, directly, in one or two sentences.
    Do not restate the question and do not explain your method.
 3. Keep units, scale and period exactly as the evidence states them. If several
    periods are quoted, use the one the question asks about.

 4. RELEVANCE AND SUFFICIENCY ARE DIFFERENT TESTS. Apply relevance first.

    RELEVANCE — are these quotes ABOUT what was asked?
    Ask: would a reader agree this quote is on the subject of the question?
    If the quotes are about a DIFFERENT subject, set `answerable` to false.
    Being from the right filing is not enough.
      Asked which supplemental indentures were entered into, and given quotes
      about adopting an accounting standard, the honest response is
      `answerable: false` — not a fluent summary of the accounting standard.
      An on-subject answer to a question nobody asked scores WORSE than
      declining, because it reads as authoritative and it is wrong.

    SUFFICIENCY — do the on-subject quotes settle it?
    Once the quotes ARE on the subject, do not hold out for more. If they
    support a partial or approximate answer, give it and say what it rests on;
    a later verification step decides whether it is enough. Do not decline
    merely because you would prefer more evidence.

 5. ⚠️ NEVER WRITE A REFUSAL INTO `answer`. To decline, set `answerable` to
    false and leave `answer` empty. An answer that opens "I cannot determine…"
    or "The provided pages do not contain…" is a refusal, and shipping it in
    the `answer` field makes it count as a WRONG ANSWER rather than an honest
    decline — the single most expensive mistake available here. The decision to
    decline is the flag; it is never prose.

 6. List in `supporting_quote_indexes` the index of every quote your answer
    actually rests on. If you cannot name at least one, you are not answering
    from the evidence — set `answerable` to false.
